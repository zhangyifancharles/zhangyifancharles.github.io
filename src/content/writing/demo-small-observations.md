Sometimes the most memorable part of a book is a small observation rather than its central argument.

It may have no immediate use. It may not connect to anything else yet. Keeping a note gives it another chance to become meaningful later.

## Between recording and understanding

Copying a sentence is a beginning. The next questions might be: why did it make me pause? Would it still hold in another setting?

![An illustration of an open book, a pencil, and reading notes](/images/reading-notes.svg)

*An original illustration accompanying this sample essay.*

A small question can change what stands out the next time we read.

## Let connections take their time

Loose notes do not need to become a system immediately. First, make each observation precise. Then notice which questions keep returning.

Sometimes a direction begins with those recurring questions.
